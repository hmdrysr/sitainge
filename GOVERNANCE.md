# Governance

## Roles
Contributors (anyone) · Native-Speaker Reviewers · Regional Reviewers · Linguistic Reviewers · Archive/Ethics Reviewers · Editorial Board · Project Steward.

Academic credentials are not the only route to participation. A native speaker's knowledge counts as evidence.

## Pipeline
Contribution, Review, Acceptance, Publication and Canonical reference are separate steps held by different roles.

## Review levels
1. Automated validation (structure only, never truth)
2. Community review (recognizability, pronunciation, meaning, naturalness, locality)
3. Linguistic review (analysis, comparative claims, etymology)
4. Editorial decision (entry into the accepted reference corpus)

## Data states
RAW, REVIEW, ACCEPTED, ARCHIVED. Nothing useful is deleted. Rejected or superseded material is archived with its decision history.

## Acceptance
Accepted means "the project has sufficient evidence to include this form." It never means "all other forms are wrong."

## Disputed forms
Kept visible with competing analyses, evidence, speakers, regions and dates.

## Regional protection
A form documented from a legitimate Chittagonian-speaking community is recorded first and evaluated second. Unfamiliarity to a reviewer is not evidence of invalidity.

## Safeguards
Protected main branch, pull requests, multiple required reviewers for foundational changes, CODEOWNERS, signed and immutable releases, changelog, decision log, reversible changes, audit trail.

## Independence
No government, organization, donor, researcher or individual holds unilateral authority over linguistic truth. Funding and politics do not determine vocabulary or conclusions.

## Conflicts of interest
Reviewers disclose direct conflicts and recuse from those decisions.
