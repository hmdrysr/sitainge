# Governance

## Roles
The roles are Contributors (anyone), Native-Speaker Reviewers, Regional Reviewers, Linguistic Reviewers, Archive/Ethics Reviewers, the Editorial Board and the Project Steward.

Academic credentials are not the only route to participation. A native speaker's knowledge counts as evidence.

## Pipeline
Contribution, review, acceptance, publication and canonical reference are separate steps, held by different roles.

## Review levels
1. Automated validation (structure only, never truth)
2. Community review (recognizability, pronunciation, meaning, naturalness, locality)
3. Linguistic review (analysis, comparative claims, etymology)
4. Editorial decision (entry into the accepted reference corpus)

## Data states
The data states are RAW, REVIEW, ACCEPTED and ARCHIVED. Nothing useful is deleted. Rejected or superseded material is archived with its decision history.

## Acceptance
Accepted means that the project has sufficient evidence to include a form. It never means that all other forms are wrong.

## Disputed forms
Disputed forms remain visible, together with the competing analyses, evidence, speakers, regions and dates.

## Regional protection
A form documented from a legitimate Chittagonian-speaking community is recorded first and evaluated second. A form that is unfamiliar to a reviewer is not, for that reason, invalid.

## Safeguards
The safeguards are a protected main branch, pull requests, multiple required reviewers for foundational changes, CODEOWNERS, signed and immutable releases, a changelog, a decision log, reversible changes and an audit trail.

## Independence
No government, organization, donor, researcher or individual holds unilateral authority over linguistic truth. Funding and politics do not determine vocabulary or conclusions.

## Conflicts of interest
Reviewers disclose direct conflicts of interest and recuse themselves from the related decisions.
