Evidence in the repository

Counts are read from the project's files. Most records remain unverified leads.

    Method in brief

    • Evidence levels. Each record is graded A to E or marked as unassessed. Levels D and E are never presented as established fact.
    • Recordings first. The documentation plan treats recordings of speakers as the primary data and written entries as derived from them. According to the learning-design notes of October 8, 2026, the repository then held about 200 unverified entries and no recordings.
    • Consent. Contributors choose whether their words and recordings are published and how they are credited. Published text is dedicated to the public domain (CC0 1.0).
    • Speaker authority. Speakers keep authority over their contributions. Consent is to be recorded for each session and may be withdrawn. A word from another language is never recorded as a siṭaiṅga form.
    • Spellings. Variant spellings are kept side by side, and no single spelling has been designated correct.
    • AI. AI is used for routine tasks such as formatting. Its output is never treated as evidence.
    • Records. Every change and deletion is publicly recorded.

    The documentation plan is a proposal dated October 8, 2026, and parts of it are not yet implemented. Dadi's lesson design is described separately in the learning design notes.

        Contribute

        No linguistic training is needed. A record is corrected by editing a file in the repository, and every tool updates from that file.

        Record words

        Add words and sentences in Dadi, using the spelling you normally use.

        Check entries

        Report any entry that is wrong or that you say differently. Speakers from every area are needed.

        Take part in an AI interview

        Answer questions from any chatbot that uses the project's interview prompt, then send the result.

        Contribute AI capacity

        Unused allowance on a personal AI account can be applied to checked work, done in small portions.

        Moderate

        Moderators review learning videos and photos so that learners see only suitable material.

        Build

        The code, data and notes are open. A hand-off guide explains how the parts fit together.
