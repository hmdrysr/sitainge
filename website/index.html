The Chittagonian language, kept by the people who speak it.

Millions of people speak it every day, and very little of it is written down. This open project gathers it, checks it with speakers, and teaches it back.

Why this exists

siṭaiṅga is a living language with no standard spelling and almost no teaching materials. Most people who speak it learned it from their grandmothers. When families move and homes change, that chain gets thinner.

This project does not replace anyone's way of speaking. It writes down what speakers say, keeps every spelling side by side, and records who said it and how sure we are. Nothing is called correct until speakers and reviewers have checked it.

Everything is public domain (CC0), so schools, researchers, writers and app builders can use it freely.

Dadi, the app

Named for the word most Chittagonians use for grandmother. Free, works offline, nothing to sign up for.

Learn

Short lessons: listen, repeat, then answer before the reveal. Words you miss come back sooner.

Type with sounds

An on-screen keyboard with every IPA symbol. Each key says its sound, so you can write a word without knowing IPA.

Translate

Turn text or a whole web page into siṭaiṅga, using only words the project holds. Anything it does not know stays in English.

Teach

Add a word, another way people say it, or a fix. It goes to the project's public files as an unverified lead.

  1. Open Dadi. On a phone, use "Add to Home Screen" to install it like any app.
  2. Practise a few words a day. The first lesson takes about three minutes.
  3. Say it your way. If a word is different where you come from, tell Dadi. That is how the dictionary improves.

How to contribute

You do not need to be a linguist. Pick what fits you.

Speak

Add words and sentences in Dadi, in any spelling you would use with a friend. There is no wrong spelling.

Check

Tell us when an entry is wrong, or when you say it differently. Speakers from every area are needed.

Be interviewed by an AI

Use any chatbot with the project's interview prompt. It asks, you answer, and you send the result.

Lend some AI time

Spare tokens on your own AI account can do careful, checked work for the project, in chunks that never get wasted.

Watch and vet

Moderators review learning videos and photos, so learners only see good material.

Build

The code, data and notes are open. A hand-off guide explains how everything works.

How it is kept, and how it moves forward

Kept

Every record is stored in open files with its full history, so nothing is lost or quietly changed. Each entry carries an evidence level and a note on who supplied it. Backups are routine, and withdrawn contributions are handled under written rules.

Moving forward

Records start as unverified leads. Speakers and reviewers move them up the evidence levels. Recordings, with separate consent, will replace machine voices. Two written forms are being developed side by side: an everyday form that anyone can type, and a formal form in IPA for documents.

AI helps with chores such as formatting and cross-checking, and its output is never treated as evidence.
