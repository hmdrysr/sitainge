# Consent form (template)

Status: draft template, October 2026. **This is a template, not legal advice. It must be reviewed by an ethics adviser before anyone uses it with a speaker.** Placeholders in square brackets are completed by the project before use.

How to use it: read the form aloud to the speaker in the language they prefer, in an unhurried way. Offer a printed copy. Where writing is a barrier, record the speaker's spoken consent at the start of the session and note this in the session log (`oral_consent_recorded`). Consent is recorded once per session and every record from that session inherits it. Nobody is pressed to say yes to any choice.

---

## About the siṭaiṅge project

The siṭaiṅge project documents the Chittagonian language (siṭaiṅga). We are asking to record you saying words, phrases or short stories so that the language is written down and heard by future learners and researchers. Taking part is voluntary. You can stop at any time, and you can skip any question.

**Who is responsible:** [name of project lead], [contact]. **Who has reviewed this form:** [ethics adviser, date].

## What we will do

- Record your voice, with a phone or recorder, and note your answers.
- Keep a short record of the session: the date, the district and upazila, and the device we used. We do not publish your name or the place where you live.
- Keep only broad details about you in public: an age band (under 30, 30 to 59, 60 and over), how you describe your gender if you choose, and the upazila you grew up in.

## Your choices

Please choose yes or no for each. Each choice is separate, and a no never affects any other choice.

| | Choice | Yes | No |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) | **Record me.** You may record my voice during this session. | | |
| (b) | **Research archive.** You may keep the recording and its transcript in a research archive, where approved researchers may use them. | | |
| (c) | **Public release.** You may publish the words and, if I also agree here, the recording for anyone to use freely under the CC0 public-domain dedication. | | |
| (d) | **Credit.** Credit me by name: [name to use] / Keep me pseudonymous (I will be known by a number only). | | |
| (e) | **AI-assisted processing.** You may use computer tools, including artificial intelligence, to help transcribe or sort my recording. A person checks any result before it is used. | | |

If you say no to (e), your recording is never given to an automated tool.

## What CC0 means

If you say yes to (c), anyone may copy and reuse what you shared, for any purpose, without asking and without paying. This cannot be undone for copies that others have already made. Choice (c) does not need to include your voice; you may allow the written words only, and say so here: [ ] words only [ ] words and recording.

## Your rights

- **Withdrawal.** You may withdraw at any time by contacting [contact]. We remove your items from public pages and from the research archive as soon as we can, and we record that you withdrew. We cannot remove copies already made by others under a CC0 release; we will tell you before you choose (c) if this applies.
- **Questions and listening.** You may ask to hear your recording and ask what has been done with it.
- **Privacy.** We keep your full details, if we hold any, on a private list outside the public repository. We do not record people other than you. If someone else's voice is captured by accident, we remove that part.
- **No payment or pressure.** [Describe any thank-you or travel costs, or state that there are none.]

## Speakers under 18

A parent or guardian signs for the child, and the child also gives their own agreement (assent) in words they understand. Both are noted in the session log.

## Notes for the ethics adviser

- Does the withdrawal wording match the legal and institutional requirements of the place where recording happens?
- Is the CC0 explanation adequate for informed consent, particularly for audio and for sacred or sensitive material?
- Are community-level permissions needed in addition to individual consent?
- The form must exist in the speaker's preferred language. Translations are made by a speaker and checked by a second person; this template does not supply them.
