# Canadian style guide for user-facing copy

Status: draft, October 8, 2026. Applies to the landing page, interface text, contribution forms, error messages, and explanatory prose.

## 1. Project rules (override everything below)

- The language is written **siṭaiṅga**. The project is written **siṭaiṅge** in headers and **Sitainge** in repository names and URLs.
- The English name of the language is **Chittagonian**. The city and region are always **Chittagong**. Never use the 2018 official respelling.
- The landing page never uses the words Bangla, Bengali, or Bengal. Where a relationship to other languages must be described, do it on a separate evidence page, with a source.
- Never invent Chittagonian. Every example form carries a source or is labelled as unverified. Do not coin glosses, spellings, or sentences for illustration.
- Do not state what has not been checked. Say what was and was not verified.

## 2. Register and voice

1. **Neutral third person.** Describe the project and the language; do not address the reader as a customer. Instructions may use the imperative ("Enter the word as you say it.").
2. **Measured claims.** Match the verb to the evidence: "is attested in", "is reported by", "appears to", "may". Reserve "is" and "are" for verified facts.
3. **Hedge where evidence is limited, and say why.** "One speaker reports this form; no second source has confirmed it." Do not stack hedges ("may possibly perhaps").
4. **Attribute.** Name the source, the year, and the type of source (census, recording, published grammar, speaker report). Distinguish mother-tongue speakers from people who know the language.
5. **Concrete nouns, plain verbs.** "Recording", "word list", "speaker", not "resources", "stakeholders", "content", "solutions".
6. **No marketing language.** Avoid "unique", "rich heritage", "vibrant", "revolutionary", "cutting-edge", "seamless", "empower", "journey", "passionate".
7. **No chatty address.** No "Hey!", "Oops!", "Let's get started!". Second person ("you") is acceptable in short instructions and errors, sparingly.
8. **Minimal exclamation marks.** None in body copy, errors, or labels. At most one per page, for a genuine confirmation.
9. **Active voice** by default. Use passive only when the actor is unknown or irrelevant ("The entry was saved.").
10. **Short sentences.** Aim for 15 to 25 words in prose; under 12 in interface text. One idea per sentence.
11. **Speakers and communities as subjects, not objects.** "Speakers in Chittagong shared these recordings", not "data was collected from respondents".

Vocabulary for people and language:

- **Indigenous** (capital I) when referring to Indigenous peoples in Canada; name the specific nation or language where known.
- **Speaker**, **speech community**, **contributor**, **reviewer**. Avoid "native informant", "respondent", "subject", "tribe".
- **Chittagonian** is the English language name; **siṭaiṅga** is the name in the language's own written form. Do not call it a "dialect" or "language" as a settled claim in interface text; use "language" per project position and put classification discussion on the evidence page.
- Use "language" not "mother tongue" unless the source specifically reports mother tongue.
- Person-first or neutral wording for disability and age. Gender-neutral wording ("they", "people", "chair").
- Names of people, organizations, and nations are capitalized as the people themselves write them.

## 4. Mechanics

**Dates and times**
- In prose and interface: month day, year, as in **October 8, 2026**. Comma after the day and, mid-sentence, after the year ("On October 8, 2026, the page was updated.").
- Month and year only: **October 2026** (no comma).
- Numeric dates (file names, data, logs): **2026-10-08** (year-month-day, hyphens). Never 08/10/2026.
- Time: **3:30 p.m.** in prose; 24-hour **15:30** acceptable in logs. Give the time zone when it matters ("3:30 p.m. EDT").
- Decades without apostrophe: 1990s. Centuries in numerals: 20th century.
- Ranges with an en dash: 1851–61 is acceptable in tables; use "from 1851 to 1861" in prose.

**Numbers**
- Spell out zero to nine in prose; use numerals for 10 and above, for measurements, ages, percentages, and anything in a table or the interface count ("3 entries").
- Never start a sentence with a numeral; rephrase.
- Thousands separator: comma in English prose (25,440); in data and code use no separator.
- Decimals use a point (3.5). Metric units; spell the unit in prose ("12 kilometres"), abbreviate in tables (km).
- Currency: **$25** for Canadian dollars; write "US$25" or "BDT 3,000" if another currency.
- Telephone: 514-555-0100.

**Punctuation**
- **Serial comma:** not required in Canadian general usage; use it only when it prevents ambiguity. Pick one rule and keep it: this project uses it only for clarity.
- **Quotation marks:** double quotation marks for quotes; single only inside double. Punctuation outside the closing mark unless it belongs to the quoted words (Canadian Press convention).
- **Apostrophes and quotes:** typographic (curly) in prose; straight in code.
- **Dashes:** spaced en dash or unspaced em dash; be consistent. This project uses the **unspaced em dash** sparingly. Prefer a comma or full stop.
- **Hyphens:** "co-operate" and "co-ordinate" are acceptable; this project uses **cooperate** and **coordinate**. Use hyphens in compound modifiers before a noun ("word-level analysis").
- **Ellipses and exclamation:** avoid. No "..." in labels, except "Loading…" using the single character.
- **Lists:** parallel structure; end items with a full stop only if they are full sentences.
- **Abbreviations:** spell out on first use ("International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)"). No full stops in acronyms. "e.g." and "i.e." only in parentheses; otherwise "for example", "that is".
- **Italics:** for words cited as words and for forms in the language, with the English gloss in double quotation marks. Show the source in a following parenthesis.

**Capitalization**
- **Interface text: sentence case** (buttons, labels, menu items, headings, table headers): "Add a recording", not "Add A Recording".
- Page titles and headings in prose documents: sentence case also, for consistency across the project. (The Encyclopedia articles read use title case for headings; the project deliberately departs from that.)
- Capitalize proper nouns only: Chittagong, Chittagonian, Canada, McGill. Do not capitalize "language", "community", "the project".
- Do not use ALL CAPS for emphasis or for buttons.
- Project name in headers: **siṭaiṅge** is lowercase even at the start of a heading or sentence. Prefer rephrasing so it does not start a sentence.

## 5. Interface microcopy rules

1. Say what happened, why, and what to do next, in that order, in one or two short sentences.
2. Buttons: verb plus object, sentence case, no full stop ("Save entry", "Submit recording").
3. Do not blame the user. Describe the system state.
4. Do not apologize in routine errors; do apologize once, plainly, for data loss risk ("Your draft may not have been saved.").
5. Do not use "please" more than once on a screen, and not before commands that are routine ("Enter a word.").
6. Never use "successfully". The confirmation is the fact: "Entry saved."
7. Empty states: say what is absent, then how to add it. No cartoon voice.
8. Evidence labels: state the source type and count, not a quality judgement ("Reported by 1 speaker", not "Weak").
9. Plain honesty about verification: "Not yet checked by a second speaker."
10. Never use jargon in an error ("null", "exception", "payload"); log it separately.

## 6. Words to avoid (South Asian English constructions and general filler)

Plain Canadian replacements are in the second column. These are common in formal South Asian business English; they read as stiff or unclear to most Canadian readers.

| Avoid | Use |
|---|---|
| kindly (as request marker) | omit, or "please" once |
| do the needful | say the action: "Upload the file." |
| revert back / revert (meaning reply) | reply; respond |
| prepone | move earlier; reschedule to an earlier time |
| updation | update |
| tell me the doubt / clear my doubt | ask a question; explain |
| "only" as emphasis ("I am here only") | omit, or "just", or restructure |
| "itself" as emphasis ("today itself") | "today"; "right away" |
| very, so, too much (overuse) | delete, or use a precise word |
| respected sir / madam, dear user | use the name, or no salutation |
| please do (command stack) | omit "please do"; use the verb |
| the same (as a pronoun: "send the same") | it, them, the form, the file |
| cope up | cope |
| discuss about | discuss |
| a lot of informations; advices; furnitures; staffs | much information; advice; furniture; staff |
| it is requested / it is informed | state who requests: "We ask you to…" |
| as per | according to; under; following |
| out of station | away; travelling |
| fill up the form | fill out / complete the form |
| pass out (graduate) | graduate |
| timepass | pastime; (omit) |
| today morning / yesterday night | this morning; last night |
| I have a doubt | I have a question |
| What is your good name? | What is your name? |
| Do one thing | (omit; give the instruction) |
| myself Iffy | I am Iffy |
| He is having a car | He has a car |
| I am understanding | I understand |
| enclosed herewith, hereby, aforementioned | attached; (omit) |
| stacked adjectives ("a very beautiful old traditional folk song") | "a traditional folk song" |
| marketing: unique, vibrant, rich, authentic, amazing | state the fact |
| over-formal openers: "With due respect", "I am writing to inform you that" | start with the point |
| "Hereby we request your goodself" | "We ask you to…" |
| "Last but not the least" | "Finally" |
| "Needful", "bye the bye", "intimate" (meaning inform) | "needed"; "by the way"; "tell" |

Also avoid generic AI-sounding filler: "delve", "tapestry", "navigate the landscape", "it's important to note", "in today's world", "seamlessly", "leverage".

## 7. Source notes (what was read; paraphrase only)

Read in full via fetch, October 8, 2026:

- https://www.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/montreal — Formal third-person encyclopedic prose; light hedging ("largely", "in part", "approximately"); numerals for statistics, "per cent" in prose; day-month-year dates in this article; metric units; title-case headings; mixed Canadian and British spellings (centre, labour, neighbourhood) plus mixed accents on Montreal.
- https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/inuktitut — Neutral third person; qualified claims ("usually", "often", "to a large degree"); speaker figures attributed to a source and year with categories distinguished; technical terms glossed inline; title-case headings; -our spellings with -ize spellings.
- https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/anishinaabemowin-ojibwe-language — Attributes origin accounts to knowledge keepers instead of asserting them; explains competing names and flags one as a misnomer; italics for forms with English glosses in quotation marks; body dates month-day-year; commas in large numbers; headings in title case with some inconsistency.
- https://www.noslangues-ourlanguages.gc.ca/en/writing-tips-plus/date-order-of-elements (Government of Canada, Writing Tips Plus, successor to The Canadian Style content) — English dates in words use month-day-year; all-numeric dates use year-month-day with hyphens; day-first not recommended.
- https://nos-langues.canada.ca/fr/writing-tips-plus/spelling-international-variations (same) — Recommends -ize, -our nouns, -re nouns, -ce for defence, enrol with one l, double l in travelled; does not rule on licence/practise, cheque, catalogue, program, grey (those entries here follow common Canadian usage and should be confirmed against the Canadian Oxford Dictionary).

Search listings seen but not read in full: https://publications.gc.ca/site/eng/9.646585/publication.html (bibliographic record for The Canadian Style, 1996 edition); https://www.btb.termiumplus.gc.ca/tpv2guides/guides/tcdnstyl/index-eng.html (index of the legacy guide, not opened).

Not read: the Canadian Press Stylebook (paid, not accessed); University of Toronto, UBC, and McGill writing guides. Rules attributed to Canadian Press or Canadian universities in sections 3 and 4 (quotation punctuation, serial comma, sentence-case UI) are common conventions stated from general knowledge, not verified in this session. Several fetches returned 404: the Encyclopedia pages on Indigenous languages in Canada, endangered languages, and its own editorial or style pages were not found, so no Encyclopedia house style rules were confirmed beyond observed practice. The Encyclopedia's own day-month-year and title-case practice differs from this guide on purpose.
