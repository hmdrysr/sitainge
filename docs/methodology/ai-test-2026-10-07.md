# AI Test Log 1: native-speaker check of AI guesses (2026-10-07)

Status: record of a test, not evidence about Chittagonian. The native speaker is the project owner (single speaker, no second-speaker verification).

## What was tested
The AI (a general-purpose model with no verified Chittagonian data, only the 2019 list and source pointers) was asked to translate 12 short English items into Chittagonian in Roman letters. The speaker marked results. The AI's own attempted forms are deliberately **not reproduced here**, so they cannot be mistaken for Chittagonian data.

## Result
- 1 of 12 correct (the word for water).
- 1 of 12 partly right (the word for boy, but not for child).
- 10 of 12 wrong.

## Failure modes found
1. **Bangla substitution.** Bangla words were given as Chittagonian: Bangla "bhat" for rice, "ghum" for sleep, "bhalo" for good, "bajar" for market. The speaker's forms are different words. This confirms hard rules 3 and 4 of the working paper.
2. **Bangla-shaped verb endings.** Continuous and past endings were built by analogy with Bangla and were wrong.
3. **One form for "you".** The AI used one word for "you" and "your". The speaker's data separates a friend form from an elder form (tui vs oney, oner).
4. **Two forms of the same verb.** The speaker's word list uses respectful or dictionary forms (haun eat, zoun go, non take). Their sentences use everyday forms (hai, zai, loi). An AI that mixes the two will produce odd sentences. Records now carry a `form_note` (D-017).
5. **Missing function words.** "Let's" was missing ("solo"). The AI could not have guessed it.
6. **Spelling treated as an error.** The AI at first called a spelling difference a "variant" of a base form. The speaker states there is no established spelling and no wrong spellings (D-015).

## Where Bangla and Chittagonian appear to overlap (hypotheses only)
The speaker's "solo" resembles Bangla "cholo", and "loi" resembles Bangla "niye" (having taken), with an l for an n. These are noted as observations for linguists. They are not claims of borrowing or common descent.

## Proposed changes to the AI working paper (for steward decision)
The working paper is changed only by a governance decision. The following changes are proposed:
- Add a rule: never mark a spelling wrong; mark only forms the speaker says are not Chittagonian.
- Add a rule: record whether a form is respectful/dictionary or everyday, and do not combine the two.
- Add a rule: IPA may be drafted only as `ai-drafted-unverified` and never entered as fact.
- Add to the interview prompt: ask about "you" with context (friend, elder, stranger) and ask for both dictionary and everyday forms of verbs.
