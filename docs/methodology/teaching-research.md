# Teaching research behind Dadi

Status: working evidence summary, 2026-10-08. Prepared for the siṭaiṅga (English name: Chittagonian) learning app, Dadi.

## How this was checked

- Every DOI marked "checked" was resolved against the Crossref record on 2026-10-08, and title, first author, venue and year matched the claim.
- Where only a URL is given, the page was found by search and the record exists, but the DOI was not machine-confirmed.
- Items I could not verify are listed at the end and are not used as evidence. Nothing here was invented to fill a gap.
- Strength labels: **Strong** (meta-analysis or replicated experiments, close to our setting), **Moderate** (good evidence, but different population or setting), **Weak** (theory, narrative review, or industry study), **Design judgement** (no direct evidence; our decision).
- Almost all L2 evidence concerns large, standardized languages with recordings and classrooms. Transfer to siṭaiṅga is by inference. Nothing below has been tested on siṭaiṅga learners.

## Claims from theory with mixed support

- **Comprehensible input (Krashen 1982) vs. output (Swain 1985) vs. interaction (Long 1996).** I did not verify these primary texts or find DOIs (books, or articles without DOIs). The synthesis in the field is that input is necessary but not sufficient; pushed output and interaction add noticing and fluency. Dadi therefore includes both listening-style input and low-stakes production, and keeps claims about "acquisition without study" out of the app.
- **Total Physical Response.** I did not find a verified controlled evaluation. Treat it as useful for concrete action vocabulary in face-to-face use, with no basis for an app to claim it works.
- **Silent Way and Te Ataarangi (Māori).** Revitalization practice with community backing; I found no verified peer-reviewed comparison, so it informs ethos (learner discovery, community ownership) only.

## Cautions that apply to Dadi specifically

1. **Unverified content.** Spaced repetition is only as good as the answer key. Wrong entries get memorised efficiently. Each entry carries a verification status and the app says so (design doc, section 9).
2. **No recordings.** Device text-to-speech for an unstandardized language may be wrong or sound like another language. It must never be presented as a model pronunciation. Prefer silence and IPA hints over a confident wrong voice.
3. **Spelling is not standardized.** Strict spelling grading would punish valid variants. Accept every attested spelling in the entry.
4. **Evidence is mostly from adult university students learning large languages.** Heritage learners and youth differ; effects here are inferred.
5. **Gamification evidence is weak,** and streak pressure can undermine autonomy (self-determination theory), so we exclude it.

## Not verified, so not used as evidence
